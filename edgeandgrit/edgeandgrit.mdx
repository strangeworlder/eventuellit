EDGE & GRIT
THE PLAYER'S BOOK
A tactile fantasy role-playing game of heroic delving, blade-work, grit, and wild magic Based on the Eventuellit, Them Deeper Bones, and FREE//FALL game engines by Petri Leinonen & Myrrys.
---
THE CORE RULES
EDGE & GRIT is a tabletop role-playing game of bold fantasy adventure. It is a conversation between the players and the guide about heroes carving their names into a dangerous, magic-haunted world. Each player controls a character, their avatar in the story, growing from a scrappy Tier 1 adventurer into a legendary Tier 5 veteran capable of crossing blades with inquisitors, outwitting ancient horrors, and standing against multi-phase monsters and dragons. The guide brings the rest of the world and its inhabitants to life, sets the stakes, and keeps the pace moving.
At the table, that conversation is paired with a tactile, physical dashboard. Instead of tracking shifting $+1$ or $-2$ modifiers with a pencil or erasing hit points on a spreadsheet, players feel their character's focus, poise, and exhaustion directly in their hands—sliding twenty-sided dice across their Character Mat and Combat Mat, pairing them with polyhedral Ability Dice, and watching Threat Dice stack up on the table as a fight intensifies.
Role-playing
Gameplay in Edge & Grit flows through role-playing and tactile dice allocation working as partners.
The guide describes the situation the characters face, and the players talk through their plans and explain how their characters act. The fiction establishes what is possible, what is at risk, and which of a character's capabilities apply. When a situation is safe or solved through a clever approach and the right gear, the conversation simply continues. When an action carries real risk, uncertainty, or opposition, the guide lays down Axis Cards or calls for the Combat Mat, turning the moment into a physical puzzle where players decide how much focus to commit to each part of the challenge.
Dice and mats
Everything in Edge & Grit happens openly and physically on the table. Each player sits in front of two dashboards—the Character Mat and the Combat Mat—supported by a shared Initiative Track and abstract Axis Cards in the centre of the table.
You will need the following components to play:
Action Pool ($5\text{d}20$): Five twenty-sided dice held in the player's hand. These represent the character's focus, time, and effort during a single combat round, exploration scene, or downtime phase.
Ability Dice ($\text{d}4$ to $\text{d}12$): Polyhedral bonus dice that sit directly in the six Ability boxes on the Character Mat, ready to be picked up and paired with $\text{d}20$s when the character's fictional approach brings that Ability into play.
Edge Dice ($\text{d}4$, $\text{d}6$, or $\text{d}8$): Dice kept in the Character Mat's Edge tray representing active combat skill, footwork, and guard.
Grit ($15$ or more points): A pool of tokens or beads kept in the Grit tray representing deeper physical endurance, willpower, and refusal to fall once technique runs out.
Threat Dice and Stakes Die: Extra $\text{d}4$, $\text{d}6$, $\text{d}8$, $\text{d}10$, and $\text{d}12$ dice kept by the table to stack next to combatants as parried blows mount, along with a shared Stakes Die (also called the Escalation Die) to ramp up the lethality of drawn-out clashes.
Basic dice mechanic
Every roll in the game has a universal Target Number (TN) of $13+$. There are no flat numerical modifiers added to a $\text{d}20$ roll. Aside from the shared Stakes Die in protracted fights, the only dice ever added to a $\text{d}20$ are the physical Ability Dice taken from the Character Mat.
Whenever any tie occurs—whether on the Initiative Track, in a contested roll, or between simultaneous effects—the players decide the order of resolution. Specific rules on Role skills, spells, or magic items override general core rules.
Axes
When role-playing puts the characters in a risky or high-stakes situation outside of combat, the guide breaks the moment down into simultaneous dimensions called Axes, placing symbol cards on the table for each part of the challenge.
Framing Outcome and Consequence Axes
The guide has wide creative freedom in how Axes are framed. Sometimes Axes represent separate physical tasks happening at once—for example, slipping past a sleeping dragon while snatching a runed key might use three Axis Cards: Speed (crossing the hall in time), Stealth (avoiding waking the dragon), and Opportunity (spotting and grabbing the key on the way).
Just as often, the guide can break a single risky action into outcome and consequence dimensions. A tense climb or an infiltration might be framed across Axes such as Succeed at the sneak, Avoid getting spotted by the guard, Don't hurt yourself on the way, and Find something interesting.
True stakes, hidden world
Whenever the guide places Axis Cards on the table, they always tell the players the true fictional stakes of failing each Axis (such as "If this Axis fails, the guard spots you" or "If this Axis fails, you take a hard fall on the way in"). Knowing the real consequences upfront allows players to make meaningful, dramatic choices about where to commit their $5\text{d}20$ pool and which risks they are willing to accept—without the guide needing to spoil hidden secrets of the world (such as who the guard is or what lies behind the door).
Resolving Axes
When resolving Axes, the game follows these steps:
Divide the Action Pool: The player divides their $5\text{d}20$ Action Pool across the Axes on the table. They may place as many or as few $\text{d}20$s onto an Axis as they wish, spread them evenly, or leave an Axis empty.
Empty Axes fail: Any Axis left with $0$ dice automatically fails on that dimension of the challenge. Leaving an optional Axis empty simply means passing up a secondary benefit, while leaving a danger Axis empty means choosing to take the consequence on the chin to guarantee success elsewhere.
Attach Ability Dice through fictional description: A player can attach at most $1$ Ability Die per $\text{d}20$ placed on an Axis. How the player describes their character's action determines which Ability Dice they can bring into play. If a player places multiple $\text{d}20$s on an Axis, they may pair them with multiple Ability Dice from the same Ability box or from different Ability boxes, provided their description justifies each one. For example, if a player places $2\text{d}20$ on an Axis and describes their character using both quick footwork (Reflex) and sharp observation (Insight or Knowledge), they may pair one Reflex Ability Die with the first $\text{d}20$ and one Insight Ability Die with the second $\text{d}20$.
Roll and read the result: The dice on each Axis are rolled separately. Each Ability Die adds its result directly to the single $\text{d}20$ it was paired with. On a standard Axis, the result is the single highest total among the $\text{d}20$s placed there. If the highest total is $13$ or higher, that Axis succeeds. A natural $20$ on a $\text{d}20$ is always a success. Once resolved, all Ability Dice return to their boxes on the Character Mat.

Hits
When a rule or the guide calls for Hits instead of a single pass or fail—such as on the Defence axis in combat, during Downtime, or in a Group Action—every individual $\text{d}20$ (plus its paired Ability Die) on that Axis that reaches $13+$ counts as $1$ Hit.
Critical success
If an Axis succeeds ($13+$) and at least two $\text{d}20$s rolled on that Axis show the exact same natural number, the character achieves a Critical success. The matching $\text{d}20$s themselves do not need to be $13$ or higher; rolling $4$, $4$, and $15$ on three $\text{d}20$s is a Critical success because the $15$ reaches the Target Number and the $4$s match.
A Critical success grants extra information, greater effect, or a spell's Critical benefit. At the guide's discretion, a player may also use a Critical success on one Axis to rescue and cancel out a failure on another Axis in the same roll.
Assisting
With the guide's approval, one character can assist another. The helper rolls on an appropriate Axis ($13+$). On a success, the helper hands one of their $\text{d}20$s ($+1\text{d}20$) into the acting character's Action Pool for the roll. A character can receive at most $+1\text{d}20$ per roll from assistance.
Group actions
When multiple characters work together on a shared task, each participating player allocates dice from their $5\text{d}20$ pool to the shared Axes. The number of Hits ($13+$) required on a Group Axis scales to $P$, the number of participating players:
Standard Group Axis: $\lceil P / 2 \rceil$ Hits ($1$ Hit for $1\text{--}2$ players, $2$ Hits for $3\text{--}4$ players, $3$ Hits for $5\text{--}6$ players).
Demanding Group Axis: $P$ Hits ($1$ Hit per participating player).
Heroic Group Axis: $2\times P$ Hits ($2$ Hits per participating player).
Player creeds
Four core creeds help players get the most out of the tactile mats and narrative freedom of EDGE & GRIT:
To do something, say how you do it.
Keep the game rolling.
Play into the world.
The character mat is a launchpad, not a cage.
To do something, say how you do it
Play in Edge & Grit is never reduced to pressing buttons on a character sheet. Instead, players describe what their character does and how they go about doing it—whether in detail ("Gi goes to the panel and gently presses his right hand against the top-right corner hoping to feel a click") or in broad strokes ("Fynn appeals to the inquisitor's vanity to talk the group past the cordon").
In Edge & Grit, describing how a character acts directly drives the mechanics. It tells the guide whether a roll is needed at all, which Axes belong on the table, and which Ability Dice, Skills, Backgrounds, or Tales can be paired with the player's $\text{d}20$s. Even on the Combat Mat, a player's description unlocks their dice: a character who describes dodging behind pillars uses Reflex on Defence, one who locks shields uses Physique, one who braces through sheer grit uses Stamina, and one who reads an enemy's fencing form uses Knowledge or Insight.
Keep the game rolling
Edge & Grit is built around momentum and bold choices. Whenever the dice hit the table, the stakes are open and clear. With $5\text{d}20$ in hand, a player can spread their dice to hedge against every risk, pour dice into a single Axis to hunt for Doubles and a Critical, or leave a secondary Axis empty to guarantee what matters most.
Players can weigh the odds, devise clever plans, parley with foes, or retreat to fight on better terms—as long as they keep making decisions, every roll drives the story forward.
Play into the world
The heart of the Edge & Grit experience is buying into the shared world together. Treating the setting, its inhabitants, and its dangers as real makes victories feel earned and close calls feel electric. At the same time, it is a collaborative team sport: backing up allies' scenes, sharing the spotlight, and playing with the energy of the table in mind is always more important than "winning" an encounter. Respect the magic circle of the game and help everyone's hero shine.
The character mat is a launchpad, not a cage
Skills, Backgrounds, and Tales on the Character Mat are narrative permissions. They establish who the character is and what extraordinary feats come naturally to them—a character with a Parkour skill or an acrobat's Background can naturally describe sprinting across a vertical wall or vaulting a chasm, and rolls those Axes for $0$ Grit.
At the same time, lacking a Skill never locks a character out of trying something bold or desperate. Any character can attempt an unfamiliar specialised task if they are willing to spend $1\text{--}3$ Grit points upfront to force the attempt through.

CHARACTERS
A character is the player's avatar in the world. Every character begins at Tier 1 with a $5\text{d}20$ Action Pool:
Choose starting Role: Select Expert ($2\text{d}6$ Edge), Fighter ($2\text{d}8$ Edge), or Magic-user ($2\text{d}4$ Edge). Place the starting Edge Dice into the Edge tray on the Character Mat, and note down the starting Role skills.
Place starting Ability Dice: A starting character gets one $\text{d}6$ and two $\text{d}4$ Ability Dice. Place them into three different Ability boxes on the Character Mat (at character creation, no Ability box may hold more than one die).
Calculate starting Grit: A character's maximum Grit is $15$ plus the highest possible die result in their Stamina or Power ($15$ with no die in either, $19$ with a $\text{d}4$, or $21$ with a $\text{d}6$). Place that many Grit tokens into the Grit tray.
Determine Background and Skills: Roll or choose a Background from the Role's table, note it down along with the weapon and practical item it gives, and choose starting Skills ($3$ for an Expert or Magic-user, $2$ for a Fighter).
If the character's role is magic-user: Determine starting spells.
Note down starting equipment: Take the standard traveller's gear, roll $3\text{d}6$ to determine how many gold pieces ($\text{gp}$) the character has, and if needed, buy extra equipment.
Decide what the character looks like and their name, and mark these down.
Abilities and Ability Dice
Abilities describe how well the character is suited to undertake particular challenges. Each of the six Ability boxes on the Character Mat can hold up to two physical Ability Dice ($\text{d}4$, $\text{d}6$, $\text{d}8$, $\text{d}10$, or $\text{d}12$).
Whenever a player places $\text{d}20$s on an Axis (including on the Combat Mat), they may pair up to $1$ Ability Die per $\text{d}20$ from any Ability box that matches how they describe their character performing the action. If a player places multiple $\text{d}20$s on the same Axis, they can even pull Ability Dice from different Ability boxes on that single Axis when their narration combines multiple attributes.
Physique (PHY): Bodily power, athletic ability, and general physicality. Used for melee attacks, parrying or blocking with brawn, athletic endeavours, heavy lifting, and feats of strength like forcing doors open.
Reflex (REF): Speed of movement and how quick one is to react. Used for combat initiative, dodging attacks, breaking away from melee, and crossing difficult terrain.
Stamina (STA): Metabolism, a measure of life-force, and general indicator of health. Used for holding breath, shaking off poisons or diseases, bracing through sheer toughness, enduring harsh weather, and determining maximum Grit.
Knowledge (KNO): Book-learning, folklore, and practical applications. Used for first aid, mechanisms, analysing an opponent's technique, and arcane scholarship. Knowledge also determines the number of memorised spells a character can hold.
Insight (INS): How in tune one is with the world and how one interacts with it with precision. Perception, intuition, reading intentions, spotting traps, and ranged weapon attacks fall under Insight.
Power (POW): Force of will and force of personality. Used for social presence, resisting mind-affecting magics, standing ground against terror, overchanneling spells, and determining maximum Grit.
Edge and Grit
A character's capacity to survive danger and push through hardship is divided into two pools on the Character Mat: Edge Dice and Grit.
Edge Dice and the rhythm of a fight
Edge Dice show a character's active combat skill, footwork, balance, and poise. As long as a character has Edge Dice left in their tray, no blow has truly cut them; they are turning strikes aside, rolling with impacts, and keeping their guard up.
In play, Edge and Threat Dice create a natural two-beat rhythm to every duel:
The opening clash: Because an incoming hit breaks every Edge Die whose rolled number (or sum of rolled numbers) is less than or equal to the attack's damage, a combatant with a large pool of Edge Dice will often see two or three low-rolling Edge Dice shatter at once in a fast, dramatic opening exchange.
The tense endgame: Once a combatant is battered down to their final $1$ or $2$ Edge Dice, it becomes much harder for a single unboosted weapon die to roll over a high Edge result. Blows clang off parries, Threat Dice stack up on the table with every near-miss, and the tension mounts until a heavy strike sweeps the Threat pool and breaks the defender's last Edge Die.
Edge is a hard breaking point: When an attack breaks a character's last Edge Die, that Edge Die absorbs the rest of the blow, and no damage from that roll spills over into Grit. A character only loses Grit from a Damage Roll if they already had $0$ Edge Dice before the Damage Roll was made.
A character's starting Role determines their initial Edge Dice ($\text{d}8$ for a Fighter, $\text{d}6$ for an Expert, and $\text{d}4$ for a Magic-user), though characters who branch into other Roles as they gain Tiers can build mixed pools of Edge Dice. All lost Edge Dice are regained completely when the character takes a breather in a safe, stress-free spot.
Grit
Grit represents raw physical endurance, willpower, and blood once a character's guard is broken. It is tracked as a single pool of points equal to: $$\text{Maximum Grit} = 15 + \text{highest possible result of Stamina or Power Ability Die}$$
All lost Grit points (along with all Edge Dice) are restored in full when the character takes a night's rest in the Exploration Frame or enters the Downtime Frame.
A character loses or spends Grit in four ways:
Damage when out of Edge: When a character already has $0$ Edge Dice remaining before a Damage Roll is made and takes damage, they lose Grit points equal to the single highest rolled Damage or Threat Die result.
Unskilled or tool-less attempts ($1\text{--}3$ Grit): If a character attempts a specialised task without a relevant Skill, Background, Tale, or proper tool, they spend $1$ to $3$ Grit points upfront to make the roll.
Retaining fading spells ($1$ Grit): When a Magic Roll results in a Disaster ($1\text{--}4$, or $5\text{--}12$ with Doubles) or a Diminished effect ($5\text{--}12$), the caster must spend $1$ Grit point or the spell vanishes from their memory until they rest.
Overchanneling: When casting a spell in light or no armour, a Magic-user who rolls a pre-allocated Knowledge or Power Ability Die on the Spell Axis immediately spends Grit points equal to the number rolled on that Ability Die.
Dropping to $0$ Grit and going down
Having $0$ Grit does not prevent a creature from having Edge Dice (such as regular opponents with $0$ Grit in their stat block). However, if any effect or cost—such as damage, venom, direct Grit drain, a spell Disaster, or Overchanneling—reduces a character's or opponent's Grit from $1$ or more down to $0$, they immediately lose all of their remaining Edge Dice.
A character or opponent goes down as soon as they have both $0$ Edge Dice and $0$ Grit points. For opponents, reaching $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit means they are defeated. For player characters, reaching $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit—whether by having their last Edge Die broken while at $0$ Grit, or by having their Grit drop to $0$ and stripping their remaining Edge Dice—means they immediately drop out of the fight, helpless and dying.
Skills, Backgrounds, and Tales
Skills, Backgrounds, and Tales never add flat modifiers or bonus dice to a roll. Instead, they establish what the character knows how to do and what actions are naturally possible for them in the fiction.
If a character has a relevant Skill, Background, Tale, or tool for a task, they have narrative permission to attempt extraordinary or specialised actions—such as running across a wall with Parkour, picking a complex lock, forging royal papers, or navigating trackless wilderness—and rolling on that Axis costs $0$ Grit. Without a matching Skill or tool, a character can still attempt a specialised task in a pinch by paying $1\text{--}3$ Grit points upfront.
Role
A character's starting Role establishes their initial training and signature Role skills. They can begin as an expert, a fighter, or a magic-user. As characters advance in Tier (see Character Growth), they can continue advancing in their starting Role or branch out into other Roles to create custom hybrid heroes. Furthermore, any character who discovers a spellbook or trains with a mentor during play can learn and cast spells—though only those trained as Magic-users possess the arcane techniques to tame magic's wilder risks.
Expert
Each expert has a particular set of skills they excel at. They seize control of the opening moments of an encounter, turn small blades into a flurry of strikes, and uncover clues and mechanisms others would miss.
Starting Edge Dice: $2\text{d}6$
Starting Skills: $3$ Skills (plus Background)
Experts begin with the following expert skills:
In perfect control: On the first round of combat, each $\text{d}20$ the expert places on the Initiative track of their Combat Mat grants $10$ instead of $5$ to their guaranteed Initiative floor ($1\text{d}20 = 10$, $2\text{d}20 = 20$, $3\text{d}20 = 25$, top of the track).
Lethal precision: When not wearing medium or full armour, small melee and small ranged weapons deal $\text{d}6$ base damage in the expert's hands instead of $\text{d}4$. In addition, when wielding a small weapon in their off-hand or throwing small ranged weapons, the expert unlocks Attack 3 as well as Attack 2 on their Combat Mat.
Specialist's reliability: Whenever the expert rolls on an Axis where their Background, a Skill, or a Tale applies (including Move rolls to break away from melee or cross difficult terrain), they may reroll any $\text{d}20$ that shows a natural result equal to or lower than their current Tier ($1$ at Tier 1, $1\text{--}2$ at Tier 2, and so on). They must keep the new result.
Understand the world: The expert can always attempt a Knowledge or Insight Axis ($13+$) to decipher an unknown script, ancient glyph, or unfamiliar mechanism without spending Grit for lacking a relevant Skill.
Fighter
The fighter is a master of the Combat Mat who can stand against fearsome monsters and hold the line. Where others are limited to single strikes, the fighter sweeps through multiple foes, wields heavy weapons at full power, and unlocks extra attacks with any weapon as they advance in Fighter Tiers.
Starting Edge Dice: $2\text{d}8$
Starting Skills: $2$ Skills (plus Background)
Fighters begin with the following fighter skills:
Combat focus: Each round when placing their $5\text{d}20$ on the Combat Mat, the fighter chooses one Axis (Initiative, Move, Defence, or one Attack axis). On that Axis for the round, the player may reroll any $\text{d}20$ that shows a natural result equal to or lower than the fighter's current Tier. They must keep the new result.
Heavy weapon mastery: When the fighter wields a heavy melee or heavy ranged weapon, they use a $\text{d}8$ as their Weapon Damage Die instead of a $\text{d}6$.
Sweeping attack: When making a melee attack on Attack 1, the fighter may place $1\text{d}20$ into the Area Effect slot on their Combat Mat to target multiple adjacent enemies within reach of the fighter and one another (up to a maximum number of targets equal to the fighter's current Edge Dice $+1$). Make a single Attack Roll ($13+$) using the $\text{d}20$s on Attack 1, including the Area Effect die. If the attack succeeds, each targeted enemy suffers a Damage Roll.
Shields are meant to be broken: While wielding a shield, if an enemy attack hits the fighter and they have $0$ Active Defence remaining to cancel it, the fighter may choose to shatter their shield before the Damage Roll is made. The attack is treated as a complete miss instead.
Veteran extra attacks: Upon reaching $3$ Tiers in Fighter, the character permanently unlocks Attack 2 on their Combat Mat with any weapon loadout (including a single weapon, a two-handed weapon, or weapon and shield). Upon reaching $5$ Tiers in Fighter, they permanently unlock Attack 3 with any weapon loadout.
Magic-user
Magic-users are vessels for living arcane formulas. While any adventurer who finds a spellbook or mentor can learn to cast a spell in the raw, only a Magic-user has the discipline to reroll faltering incantations, cast spells safely as rituals, and burn their own Grit to Overchannel spells into Miracles or pull them back from the brink of Disaster.
Starting Edge Dice: $2\text{d}4$
Starting Skills: $3$ Skills (plus Background)
Magic-users begin with the following magic-user skills:
Travelling spellbook and memorised spells: A magic-user starts with a travelling spellbook containing $3$ Beginner Spells plus bonus spells determined by their Knowledge Ability Die. They can hold the same number of spells ($3 + \text{Knowledge bonus}$) in their memory at one time, and can swap memorised spells during a night's rest or in Downtime.

Arcane focus: Whenever the magic-user rolls $\text{d}20$s on a Spell Axis, they may reroll any $\text{d}20$ that shows a natural result equal to or lower than their current Tier. They must keep the new result.
Overchannel: When casting a spell in light or no armour, the magic-user may place a Knowledge or Power Ability Die from their Character Mat onto the Spell Axis during the declaration phase. After rolling the $\text{d}20$s, the caster decides whether to roll that Ability Die and add its result to their highest $\text{d}20$. If they roll it, they immediately spend Grit points equal to the result shown on the Ability Die. Overchanneling is the primary way to push a spell total to $21+$ for a Miracle, and can also be used to push a $1\text{--}4$ roll or a Diminished roll with Doubles out of the Disaster range.
Ritual casting: By spending $20$ minutes outside of combat reading a spell directly from their spellbook, the magic-user can cast it as a ritual. They roll their $5\text{d}20$ on the Spell Axis as normal ($13+$), but the spell cannot trigger a Disaster (any Disaster result is treated as a Diminished $5\text{--}12$ result instead), and a Diminished result does not cause the spell to fade from memory or cost Grit to retain.
Detect magic: Unless wearing full armour, a magic-user automatically senses the presence of nearby magical items or effects. They can roll an Insight Axis ($13+$) at $0$ Grit cost to identify the type of magic and the exact location of its source.
Background
The roles are broad mechanical foundations for the characters. A fighter might be a hunter, a blacksmith, a river pirate, or a knight. A witch, oracle, archivist, or hedge-scholar could better describe what a magic-user actually does. Experts come from all walks of life—archaeologists, actors, thieves, spies, and more. This is the character's Background.
Backgrounds represent where the character comes from and what they have lived through. Whenever a task relates directly to a character's Background, rolling on that Axis requires no Grit expenditure. A character also gets a signature weapon and a practical item that reflect where they come from.
Starting equipment
In addition to the items provided by their Background, each character gets a complete set of traveller's gear: Backpack, Bedroll, Waterskin, Torches ($5$), Food (preserved, $1$ week), Flint and Steel, and Rope ($15\text{ m}$). They also get $3\text{d}6$ gold pieces ($\text{gp}$) to purchase additional equipment.

CHARACTER GROWTH
Character growth is measured in Tiers, running from Tier 1 (early-career adventurer) to Tier 5 and beyond (legendary veteran). Characters advance a Tier between adventures or by placing $1\text{d}20$ on the Training & Advancement axis during the Downtime Frame.
When a character gains a Tier, update their Character Mat as follows:
Increase character Tier by $+1$: This increases the natural $\text{d}20$ numbers the character can reroll with their Role's reroll skill (Specialist's reliability, Combat focus, or Arcane focus).
Upgrade or add an Ability Die: Choose one of the six Abilities on the Character Mat. Either place a new $\text{d}4$ Ability Die into that box (to a maximum of $2$ dice per Ability box), or step up an existing Ability Die in that box by one size: $$\text{d}4 \to \text{d}6 \to \text{d}8 \to \text{d}10 \to \text{d}12$$
Update Grit and memorised spells: Recalculate maximum Grit ($15 + \text{highest possible result in Stamina or Power}$). If the character increased their Knowledge Ability Die, their memorised spell capacity increases accordingly.
Choose a Role advancement (Single-Role or Multiclass): A character is never locked into their starting Role. Each time they gain a Tier, the player chooses which Role (Expert, Fighter, or Magic-user) to take this Tier's advancement in, reflecting how the character has grown in play. Taking a Tier in a second Role grants that Role's per-Tier advancement below (creating custom hybrid Edge pools such as $2\text{d}6 + 1\text{d}8$), though it does not grant that Role's starting Tier 1 Role skills:
Expert advancement: Gain $+1\text{d}6$ Edge Die and $+2$ new Skills.
Fighter advancement: Gain $+1\text{d}8$ Edge Die and $+1$ new Skill. Upon reaching $3$ Fighter Tiers, unlock Attack 2 with any weapon loadout; upon reaching $5$ Fighter Tiers, unlock Attack 3 with any weapon loadout.
Magic-user advancement: Gain $+1\text{d}4$ Edge Die, $+1$ new Skill, and $+1$ new spell in a spellbook.
Record a Tale: Write down a new Tale based on the character's recent adventures.
Tales
Characters who reach a new Tier gain a Tale based on what they have been up to. In mechanical effect, a Tale works just like a Background or Skill: it is tied to a memorable feat, event, or location from the party's previous adventures—"The Sneak of the Hall of the Shadow Men," "The Carrier of Fallen Friends," "The Slayer of the Northbrook Basilisk." Tales establish new narrative permissions for the character and allow them to roll related Axes without spending Grit.
On special abilities and learning magic in play
Acquiring unusual techniques, rare lore, arcane spells, or loyal companions is handled through play and conversation alongside Tier progression. If a Fighter or Expert finds an ancient tome or wins the mentorship of a witch, they can spend time in Downtime or the Exploration Frame studying to transcribe and memorise spells. Likewise, if a character wants to learn a secret martial style or befriend a beast in the wilds, they seek out a teacher or earn the animal's trust in the story.

EQUIPMENT
Coin
Coins hold value according to their material. One gold piece ($\text{gp}$) equals $10$ silver pieces ($\text{sp}$) or $100$ copper pieces ($\text{cp}$). For simplicity, standard equipment prices are listed in gold pieces ($\text{gp}$) and silver pieces ($\text{sp}$).
Weapons
Weapons are divided into melee weapons used in close quarters and ranged weapons launched at a distance. They are also categorised as bladed or bludgeoning weapons, as some monsters have resistance or vulnerability to one or the other.
On the Combat Mat, Attack 1, Attack 2, and Attack 3 are single-target attack axes. Unless modified by the Area Effect slot (such as through a Fighter's Sweeping attack), each Attack axis targets a single opponent.
A player may place as many $\text{d}20$s from their $5\text{d}20$ Action Pool onto an unlocked Attack axis as they wish ($1\text{d}20$ up to $5\text{d}20$). Even when a character has Attack 2 or Attack 3 unlocked, they are never forced to split their dice; they can pour all of their attack dice into Attack 1 for a single reliable strike, or split them across multiple Attack axes to make separate attacks against the same foe or different foes.
Unarmed or improvised weapons use Attack 1 and have a $\text{d}4$ Damage Die.
Small melee weapons include daggers and knives. They deal $\text{d}4$ damage ($\text{d}6$ for an Expert in light or no armour) and use Attack 1. When held in the off-hand, a small melee weapon unlocks Attack 2 (and Attack 3 if dual-wielding two small weapons, or for an Expert).
Normal melee weapons include staffs, clubs, swords, short spears, and axes. They deal $\text{d}6$ damage in one hand and use Attack 1. They can be paired with a shield, a free hand for casting spells, or an off-hand small weapon.
Long melee weapons are long spears, halberds, and polearms. They require two hands, deal $\text{d}6$ damage, and use Attack 1. A character wielding a long melee weapon has enough reach to strike from behind an ally or from $2\text{--}3\text{ m}$ away without entering the enemy's melee reach, meaning they do not need to roll on Move to walk away.
Heavy melee weapons are mauls, zweihänders, and greataxes. They require two hands, use Attack 1, and deal $\text{d}6$ damage ($\text{d}8$ for a Fighter).
Small ranged weapons are slings and throwing knives. They deal $\text{d}4$ damage ($\text{d}6$ for an Expert) and unlock Attack 1 and Attack 2 (plus Attack 3 for an Expert).
Normal ranged weapons are bows and light crossbows. They require two hands, deal $\text{d}6$ damage, and unlock Attack 1 and Attack 2.
Heavy ranged weapons are heavy crossbows and arbalests. They require two hands, use Attack 1, and deal $\text{d}6$ damage ($\text{d}8$ for a Fighter). A heavy ranged weapon must be reloaded between shots by spending $1\text{d}20$ on Other Actions or Move.

GAMEPLAY FRAMES
In EDGE & GRIT sessions, the flow and rhythm of the game adapt to fit what the heroes are doing. At times, weeks pass in a few sentences as characters pursue personal ambitions in a safe haven. At others, an afternoon of city investigation unfolds in a montage of key scenes, or play zooms into real-time exploration and high-speed tactical clashes. To give each moment the right pacing, the game uses four gameplay frames: Downtime, Exploration, Delve, and Tactical.
Every frame uses the same core engine—conversation first, and dividing the $5\text{d}20$ Action Pool across Axes ($13+$) when stakes are on the line—while changing how time at the table maps to time in the story.
Frame	Time Scale	Conversation & Table Play	Scope	Recovery

Downtime Frame
The Downtime Frame is a broad-strokes approach to what happens between expeditions when the party rests in a safe haven. Entering the Downtime Frame immediately restores all lost Grit points and Edge Dice, and removes all Threat Dice.
During Downtime, each player divides their $5\text{d}20$ Action Pool (attaching relevant Ability Dice) across any of the Downtime Axes below. Every die that reaches $13+$ counts as $1$ Hit:
Acquiring & trading (POW, INS, or KNO): Used when hunting for a rare or specific item, including magic items. $1$ Hit finds a seller at the standard price; $2+$ Hits reduces the price by $25%\text{--}50%$.
Rumours, lore & investigation (KNO, INS, or POW): The player names a subject they are researching. Each Hit ($13+$) allows the player to ask the guide one direct question about the subject and receive an honest, useful answer (a Critical adds one extra question or a volunteer revelation).
Arcane study (KNO or POW): $1+$ Hits uncovers the properties and command word of a magic item, transcribes a Beginner Spell into a spellbook, or lets a caster craft a single-use rune (scroll) or capsule (potion). $2+$ Hits halves the material cost or imbues the item with a Critical effect.
Crafting & mending (PHY, INS, or KNO): $1$ Hit repairs all broken mundane gear and shattered shields for the group; $1\text{--}3$ Hits crafts a specialised item from rare materials.
Connections & factions (POW or INS): $1$ Hit improves standing with a faction or cools down hostility from rivals; $2+$ Hits secures tangible backing, safe passage, or introductions.
Training & advancement (Any Ability): Placing $1\text{d}20$ here (no roll required) completes a character's Tier advancement. Alternatively, $1$ Hit swaps an existing Skill or spell, or advances training with a mentor.
Personal project (Any Ability): The player states a custom long-term goal. $1$ Hit achieves it with a minor complication or ticks $1$ segment on a $3\text{--}6$ part project clock; $2+$ Hits achieves a clean, lasting benefit.
The Exploration Frame
The Exploration Frame is used when the party is active in the wider world and each cycle of actions takes a few hours—whether investigating a conspiracy in a city, spending an afternoon at a secluded monastery, navigating court intrigue, or travelling across the wilderness.
Characters can split up to pursue separate leads using their individual $5\text{d}20$ pools, or tackle shared obstacles together using Group Action scaling.
Rest
Stopping in a safe place for a full night's rest restores all lost Edge Dice and all Grit points to their maximum, removes all Threat Dice, and allows spellcasters to change which spells they have memorised from their spellbook.
The Delve Frame
The Delve Frame is used when the group explores a dangerous location room by room or engages in immediate face-to-face encounters. Time runs $1:1$ with real time at the table, and conversations are spoken live. While moving in the Delve Frame, keep track of the group's marching order using tokens or miniatures.
Dangers and traps are telegraphed through clues in the environment. When characters solve a problem through clever use of their equipment, surroundings, Skill, or Background, no roll is needed. If they trigger a hazard or attempt something risky under pressure, the guide lays down Axis Cards ($13+$) and states the stakes.
Take a breather
A character can stop for a quiet, stress-free moment in a safe spot to catch their breath, reset their stance, and check their gear. Taking a breather restores all lost Edge Dice and removes all Threat Dice. A stabilised character at $0$ Grit recovers to $1$ Grit point and $1$ Edge Die.
The Tactical Frame
The Tactical Frame is used for combat and split-second hazards. While each round represents roughly $10$ seconds of frantic action in the fiction—leaving characters time for only a few shouted words mid-clash—play at the table is highly collaborative.
Instead of waiting around the table for single-action turns, all players plan together simultaneously during the declaration step, dividing their $5\text{d}20$ pools across their Combat Mats to coordinate movement, Active Defence, multiple attacks, and spells. Because a character can move, defend themselves and their allies, strike multiple times, and take an extra action all within a single $5\text{d}20$ allocation, one round of the Tactical Frame packs in the action and tactical decisions of two or three rounds in traditional turn-based games.

COMBAT
Combat is the signature Tactical Frame situation where the characters cross blades and spells with dangerous foes.
The Edge and Grit system naturally creates two speeds of combat:
Fast, cinematic skirmishes ($0$ Grit foes): Regular opponents (such as goblins, bandits, witch-hunters, and insect soldiers) rely on Edge Dice with $0$ Grit. They require zero hit point bookkeeping and resolve in fast, decisive $1\text{--}2$ round clashes.
Multi-round tactical set pieces (Grit-backed foes and Bosses): Lieutenants, hulking beasts, relentless undead, and multi-phase bosses bring pools of Grit points, multiple Initiative ticks, or telegraphed room-wide axes that demand teamwork, positioning, and resource management across multiple rounds.
Not every creature wants to fight to the death. The guide determines how opponents react—and when they parley, surrender, or flee—through role-playing, the situation at hand, and the opponent's Sentience:
Alien beings possess otherworldly or hyper-calculative minds that anticipate mortal reactions, declaring their actions in Step 3 after the players have committed their dice.
Sapient and Reasoning foes weigh their odds. They demand surrender when they have the upper hand, and will break, retreat, or bargain when their leader falls or the tide turns against them.
Instinctual beasts hunt for food or defend territory, retreating once seriously hurt unless cornered or protecting their young.
Non-sentient beings (undead, constructs, and oozes) never parley and fight until destroyed.
Combat sequence
The shared Initiative Track counts down from the top of the round to zero through eight boxes: $$\text{Start of Round} \to 24 \to 21 \to 19 \to 14 \to 9 \to 4 \to 0\text{ (End of Round)}$$
Each round of combat follows these steps:
Regular opponents declare: The guide places tokens for all non-sentient, instinctual, reasoning, and sapient opponents onto the Initiative Track and announces their intended actions and targets. When multiple opponents of the same type are present, the guide distributes them across their listed Initiative tick and lower ticks so that attacks come in waves.
Players plan together and place dice: Seeing what regular foes intend to do, the players discuss their team strategy at the table, declare their actions and targets, and simultaneously place their $5\text{d}20$ Action Pool (along with any Ability Dice, including pre-allocated Overchannel dice) onto their Combat Mats. If a player has at least $2\text{d}20$ available, they may discard $1\text{d}20$ for the round to delay their declaration until after Step 3.
Alien opponents declare: After the players have locked in their mats, the guide announces the actions, targets, and initiative placements of opponents with Alien sentience.
Roll Initiative: Players roll the dice placed on their Initiative axis and place their tokens on the Initiative Track.
Resolve actions in Initiative order: Counting down from Start of Round to $0$, each combatant resolves their movement and actions on their tick.
End of round bookkeeping: Check if the Stakes Die steps up.
Combat initiative
The Initiative track on the Combat Mat has five slots marked $5$, $10$, $15$, $20$, and $25$. The player rolls the $\text{d}20$s placed on Initiative (attaching an appropriate Ability Die, typically Reflex). The character's Initiative value is whichever is higher: the guaranteed floor from the number of dice placed, or the highest single die total: $$\text{Initiative} = \max\left(5 \times \text{number of }\text{d}20\text{s on Initiative},; \text{highest single die total}\right)$$
On the first round of combat, an Expert multiplies the number of $\text{d}20$s placed on Initiative by $10$ instead of $5$. After rolling, a player may always choose to take a lower Initiative number than their result. On a tie, players act before opponents in whatever order the players prefer.
Changing circumstances
Combat is dynamic, and what happens earlier in the round can make a character's declared main action impossible—for example, if an ally defeats the character's declared target before the character's turn, or if the character fails a Move roll and gets caught in melee out of reach of their target.
In these cases, the character forfeits their main action for the round (they may still complete any valid movement) and is immediately slung to the top of the Initiative Track (Start of Round) for the next round.
Tactical movement
The Move track on the Combat Mat has five slots marked $6\text{ m}$, $12\text{ m}$, $18\text{ m}$, $24\text{ m}$, and $30\text{ m}$. Each $\text{d}20$ placed on Move lets the character move up to $6\text{ m}$ ($4\text{ m}$ in full armour). Moving across unobstructed ground without leaving an enemy's reach requires no roll.
Getting caught in a melee
A player must roll the $\text{d}20$s placed on their Move track (pairing relevant Ability Dice such as Reflex or Physique) against TN $13+$ if their movement path crosses difficult terrain (rubble, ice, deep mud) or leaves or passes through an active enemy's reach.
If the Move roll fails ($12$ or lower) while trying to leave or slip past an enemy's reach, the character gets caught in the melee and must stop their movement immediately in front of that enemy. Regular opponents cannot walk past an active character's reach without stopping to engage them.
Defence and Active Defence
When a player places $\text{d}20$s on the Defence track, they roll them on their turn (pairing up to $1$ Ability Die per $\text{d}20$ based on how they describe defending—such as Reflex to dodge, Physique to parry, Stamina to weather the blow, or Knowledge/Insight to anticipate the enemy's form). Every individual die that reaches $13+$ counts as $1$ Hit and is moved into the Active Defence holding box on the Combat Mat. If the character is wielding a shield and places at least $1\text{d}20$ on Defence, they gain $+1$ bonus Active Defence automatically.
Active Defence dice remain in the holding box from the character's current turn until their next turn on the following round. A character can spend $1$ Active Defence to:
Turn $1$ successful enemy attack against them into a miss.
Turn $1$ successful enemy attack against an adjacent ally within reach into a miss.
Ward off or reduce a telegraphed breath, gaze, ray, or area effect.
Intercept and stop an enemy attempting to move past their reach.
Attack Rolls
To attack with a weapon, the player rolls the $\text{d}20$s placed on an unlocked Attack axis (Attack 1, Attack 2, or Attack 3), pairing up to $1$ Ability Die per $\text{d}20$ as justified by their description (typically Physique for melee and Insight or Physique for ranged attacks, though a player can combine Ability Dice from different boxes on a multi-die attack when their narration supports it), plus the Stakes Die if active.
Hit ($13+$): The attack connects. Make a Damage Roll using the weapon's Damage Die plus all Threat Dice currently on the target (and the Stakes Die, if active).
Pressuring miss ($12$ or lower): Even if an Attack Roll fails to reach $13+$, as long as at least $1\text{d}20$ was placed on the Attack axis, the attacker still triggers a Damage Roll using only the Threat Dice currently sitting on the target.
Other Actions
The Other Actions box on the Combat Mat is used for casting spells, drinking or administering a potion, shaking off poison on a Stamina axis ($13+$), breaking free from a pin on a Physique axis ($13+$), extinguishing flames, reloading a heavy ranged weapon, digging an item out of a backpack, or stabilising a dying friend.
Damage Rolls, Edge Dice, Threat Dice, and Grit
Players never make passive, out-of-turn Saving Throws against monster attacks. As long as an attack is cancelled with Active Defence or fails to break an Edge Die or deal Grit damage, any secondary venom, knockback, or grab on that attack is turned aside. Room-wide effects declared by monsters (such as a Floating Eye's stare or Draconic terror) place an Axis Card ($13+$) on the table for players to allocate dice to during declaration.
When an attack hits (or a miss triggers existing Threat Dice), follow these four steps:
Roll Damage and Threat Dice (keep single highest):
On a Hit ($13+$), roll the attack's Weapon Damage Die together with all Threat Dice currently on the target (plus the Stakes Die, if active).
On a Miss ($12$ or lower), roll only the Threat Dice currently on the target.
Identify the single highest number rolled among those dice. Only that single highest number is used.
Roll and compare Edge Dice ($\text{Damage} \ge \text{Edge}$):
If the defender has at least $1$ Edge Die remaining, the defender rolls all of their current Edge Dice.
If the attacker's single highest Damage/Threat result is greater than or equal to ($\geq$) an Edge Die—or greater than or equal to the sum of multiple Edge Dice—those beaten Edge Dice are broken and removed from the defender's tray. Because ties go to the damage roll ($\geq$), rolling the maximum number on a die (such as a $6$ on a $\text{d}6$) always breaks an Edge Die of the same size.
Choosing which Edge Dice break (maximum dice broken): When multiple combinations of rolled Edge Dice could be broken by the damage number, the defender chooses which Edge Dice to remove, provided they always choose a combination that breaks the maximum possible number of Edge Dice. If two Edge Dice of different sizes (such as a $\text{d}6$ and a $\text{d}8$ in a multiclass pool) roll numbers where either single die would satisfy the break count, the defender may choose to break the smaller die size and keep the larger one.
Edge is a hard breaking point: When an attack breaks a defender's last Edge Die, that Edge Die absorbs the rest of the blow—no damage from that roll spills over into Grit.
If the defender already had $0$ Edge Dice before the Damage Roll was made, skip this step and apply the single highest rolled number directly to their Grit in Step 4.
Threat Dice escalation or sweep:
If the defender lost $0$ Edge Dice and $0$ Grit points from the roll: on a Hit ($13+$), place the Weapon Damage Die that was rolled for the attack into the defender's Threat Dice pool. On a Miss ($12-$), no Weapon Damage Die was rolled, so no new die is added to the Threat pool.
If the defender lost at least $1$ Edge Die or $1$ Grit point, remove all Threat Dice currently on the defender.
Grit loss, $0$ Grit strip, and defeat:
A defender only loses Grit from a Damage Roll if they already had $0$ Edge Dice before the Damage Roll was made. In that case, subtract Grit points equal to the single highest rolled Damage/Threat number from their Grit pool.
Dropping to $0$ Grit strips all Edge Dice: If any effect or cost (such as damage, venom, direct Grit drain, a spell Disaster, or Overchanneling) reduces a character's or opponent's Grit from $1$ or more down to $0$, they immediately lose all of their remaining Edge Dice.
Going down at $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit: A character or opponent goes down as soon as they have both $0$ Edge Dice and $0$ Grit points. Opponents at $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit are defeated; player characters at $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit immediately drop out of the fight, helpless and dying.
Resistance, immunity, and vulnerability
Resistance: Add an extra $+\text{d}6$ to the Damage Pool, roll, and ignore the single die showing the highest result before comparing the remaining highest number against Edge or Grit. Do not add the extra $\text{d}6$ to Threat Dice.
Vulnerability: Add an extra $+\text{d}6$ to the Damage Pool, roll, and ignore the single die showing the lowest result before comparing the highest number against Edge or Grit. Do not add the extra $\text{d}6$ to Threat Dice.
Immunity: Remove the weapon's Damage Die from the roll completely. Any Threat Dice already on the target from previous hits are still rolled on a successful Attack Roll, as old wounds can still be jarred open.
Ongoing damage, poison, and disease
Ongoing damage: When a character is set on fire or coated in acid, place an Ongoing Damage Die ($\text{d}4$ mild, $\text{d}6$ fire, $\text{d}8\text{--}\text{d}10$ intense) beside them. At the end of each turn they take until the effect is put out (by spending $1\text{d}20$ on Other Actions or diving into water), roll the Ongoing Damage Die against their Edge and Grit. Ongoing Damage Dice are never added to a target's Threat Dice pool if shrugged off.
Poison: Venomous attacks only take effect if the attack breaks at least $1$ Edge Die or deals Grit damage. A poisoned character suffers the venom's effect until they succeed on a Stamina Axis ($13+$) under Other Actions or receive neutralising magic or antitoxins.
Disease: Contagions (such as Zombie plague after losing Grit to a zombie bite) require a Stamina Axis ($13+$) roll during the Delve or Exploration Frame after the onset time passes.
Death and dying
When a character reaches both $0$ Edge Dice and $0$ Grit points in combat—whether by having their last Edge Die broken while at $0$ Grit, or by having their Grit drop from $1$ or more down to $0$ and stripping their remaining Edge Dice—they immediately drop out of the fight, helpless and dying. At the start of the next round, place their token at the bottom of the Initiative Track ($0$).
If an ally reaches the dying character and spends an action on Other Actions before the initiative order reaches $0$, the fallen character is stabilised at $1$ Grit point (remaining helpless until they take a breather or receive magical healing).
If the round reaches $0$ before anyone stabilises them, the character becomes apparently dead. Immediately after the battle, a comrade can attempt first aid by rolling a First Aid Axis ($13+$) using Knowledge. On a success, the fallen comrade revives with $1$ Grit point and records a permanent scar as a new Tale. On a failure, the character is gone, beyond any help.
Upped stakes
When combat drags on without decisive blows, everyone eventually makes mistakes. If an entire round of combat finishes and no Edge Die was broken and no Grit points were lost by any combatant, the guide places the shared Stakes Die (or Escalation Die) in the centre of the table at $\text{d}4$.
At the end of each subsequent round in which no Edge Die is broken and no Grit is lost, step the Stakes Die up by one size: $$0 \to \text{d}4 \to \text{d}6 \to \text{d}8 \to \text{d}10 \to \text{d}12 \to \text{d}20$$
While active, the Stakes Die is rolled alongside every Attack Roll (adding to the highest $\text{d}20$) and every Damage Roll (rolled as an extra die in the Damage Pool, though never added to Threat Dice) by both characters and opponents. The Stakes Die resets to $0$ when the combat ends.

MAGIC
Magic is wild, living, and potent. Casting a spell requires the character to have memorised the magical form of the spell from a spellbook. The spellcaster must be able to speak the incantation and perform arcane gestures with at least one free hand. Spells cannot be cast while wearing full armour. Unless stated otherwise, a caster can dismiss an ongoing spell they cast at any time.
Open spellcasting vs. the Magic-user's mastery
Spellcasting is not locked behind class red tape: any Fighter or Expert who discovers a spellbook, scroll, or mentor in play (or takes a Tier advancement in Magic-user) can learn, memorise, and cast spells on a Spell Axis ($13+$). A non-Magic-user can hold a number of spells in their memory equal to their Knowledge Ability Die bonus ($\text{d}4 = 1$, $\text{d}6 = 2$, $\text{d}8 = 3$, $\text{d}10 = 4$, $\text{d}12 = 5$, plus $+1$ for a second Knowledge die; minimum $1$ spell if they have learned a spell).
However, dabbling in raw magic without a Magic-user's training is a dangerous gamble:
A dabbler rolls only their raw $\text{d}20$s on the Spell Axis without Arcane focus rerolls, meaning low rolls and Doubles on $5\text{--}12$ frequently trigger Disasters.
Without Ritual casting, a dabbler cannot safely cast utility spells out of combat without risking Disasters or losing the spell.
Without Overchannel, a dabbler cannot attach a Knowledge or Power Ability Die to rescue a Disaster or push a spell total to $21+$ for a Miracle.
Casting a spell
To cast a memorised spell, follow these steps:
Allocate dice: During declaration, place $1$ to $5\text{d}20$ from the Action Pool onto a Spell Axis (Other Actions or Attack 1 on the Combat Mat).
Pre-allocate Overchannel die (Magic-users only, optional): If a Magic-user is in light or no armour and wants the option to Overchannel the spell, they must also place a Knowledge or Power Ability Die from their Character Mat onto the Spell Axis during declaration.
Roll $\text{d}20$s: On their turn, the caster rolls the allocated $\text{d}20$s (applying Arcane focus rerolls if they have that skill).
Decide on Overchannel: After seeing the $\text{d}20$ results, a Magic-user who pre-allocated a Knowledge or Power Ability Die decides whether to roll it:
If they choose not to roll it, return the Ability Die to the Character Mat at $0$ Grit cost.
If they roll it, add its result to the highest $\text{d}20$ and immediately spend Grit points equal to the number rolled on that Ability Die. Overchanneling can push a $1\text{--}4$ roll or a $5\text{--}12$ roll with Doubles into a higher tier to prevent a Disaster, or push a strong roll to $21+$ for a Miracle.
Spell outcomes
Every spell uses the same five outcome tiers anchored around TN $13+$. Because a caster can reach $21+$ (via Overchannel) and roll Doubles on the same cast, Critical upgrades the spell's raw power while Miracle expands its utility, targets, or duration, allowing both to combine when both conditions are met.

Spells and damage
Enemies never roll Saving Throws against spells; the caster's Spell Roll determines the outcome directly. Direct attack spells (Elemental burst, Energy bolt) roll their damage against Edge and Grit using the standard Damage Roll rules, adding $1$ rolled spell damage die to the target's Threat pool if the target loses $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit. Magical bonus damage dice added to weapons (Fangs of frost, Growth) are never added to Threat Dice.
Spell types
There are eight spell types. Spirit, Water, Air, Earth, and Fire form the five elemental types. Light spells manifest wishes and wards, Dark spells manifest dread and entropy, and Void spells channel anti-magic and emptiness. If a spell lists multiple types, the caster chooses or rolls one version when learning it.

OPPONENTS
Opponents use the same core combat rules as characters, while scaling encounter pacing between fast skirmishes ($0$ Grit foes) and multi-round boss fights (Grit-backed foes):
Attack Pools ($13+$): Each opponent lists the $\text{d}20$s (and any Ability Die) they roll to hit against TN $13+$, along with their Weapon Damage Die. Like player attacks, an opponent hits on $13+$ (rolling their Damage Die + the target's Threat Dice) and triggers existing Threat Dice on the target even on a miss ($12-$).
Edge, Grit, and defeat: Regular skirmishers have Edge Dice and $0$ Grit, allowing them to be defeated as soon as their Edge Dice break without tracking hit points. Lieutenants, large monsters, undead, and bosses have Grit pools as well. When an attack breaks an opponent's last Edge Die, that Edge Die absorbs the rest of the blow—no damage from that roll spills over into Grit, and an opponent only loses Grit from a Damage Roll if they already had $0$ Edge Dice before the roll was made. However, if any effect reduces an opponent's Grit from $1$ or more down to $0$, they immediately lose all of their remaining Edge Dice. An opponent is defeated as soon as they have both $0$ Edge Dice and $0$ Grit points.
Opponent rerolls: Any opponent whose Edge Dice are $\text{d}8$ or larger may reroll any natural $\text{d}20$ result equal to or lower than the number of Edge Dice they currently have left.
Initiative waves: An opponent's listed Initiative tick is their maximum standard speed. When fielding multiple opponents of the same type, the guide distributes them across their listed tick and lower ticks ($19\rightarrow 14\rightarrow 9\rightarrow 4$).

QUICK REFERENCE
Target Number: $13+$ for all rolls. Natural $20$ always succeeds. Ties are decided by the players.
Standard Axis: Roll $\text{d}20$s (+ at most $1$ Ability Die per $\text{d}20$, pulled from any Ability box justified by the player's description). Keep the single highest total.
Hits Axis (Defence, Downtime, Group Actions): Every $\text{d}20$ (+ paired Ability Die) reaching $13+$ counts as $1$ Hit.
Critical: Axis reaches $13+$ and at least two $\text{d}20$s on that Axis show the same natural number.
Unskilled / tool-less attempt: Spend $1\text{--}3$ Grit upfront.
Initiative: $\max(5 \times \text{dice on Initiative},; \text{highest roll})$. Experts use $10 \times \text{dice}$ on Round 1.
Move: $6\text{ m}$ per die ($4\text{ m}$ in full armour). Roll $13+$ only for difficult terrain or leaving/passing through enemy reach (failure stops movement in melee).
Damage sequence:
Hit ($13+$): Roll Weapon Damage Die + target's Threat Dice (+ Stakes Die). Miss ($12-$): Roll only target's Threat Dice. Take the single highest number rolled.
Edge ($\geq$): If defender has $\geq 1$ Edge Die, roll Edge Dice. Any Edge Die (or sum of Edge Dice) $\leq$ the highest Damage/Threat number breaks. Defender chooses which Edge Dice break, but must break the maximum possible number of Edge Dice. Breaking the last Edge Die absorbs the rest of that blow—no damage from that roll spills over into Grit.
Threat: If target lost $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit, add the rolled Weapon Damage Die to target's Threat pool (none added on a miss). If target lost $\geq 1$ Edge or Grit, clear all Threat Dice.
Grit & Defeat: A defender only loses Grit from a Damage Roll if they already had $0$ Edge Dice before the roll was made (subtract the highest Damage/Threat number from Grit). If any effect or cost reduces Grit from $1+$ down to $0$, immediately remove all remaining Edge Dice. At both $0$ Edge and $0$ Grit, an opponent is defeated and a character is dying (tick $0$ next round).
